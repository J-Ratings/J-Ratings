    Mali had taken in the simultaneous match against Malawi, both
    Angola and Algeria played their second half in the knowledge that 
    a (0-0) draw would most likely qualify both, and were thereby in  
    a situation comparable to that of West Germany and Austria at the 
    1982 World Cup, in which the result of their match (1-0 to West
    Germany) eliminated Algeria.  When Egypt and Cameroon arranged a
    0-0 draw at the African Youth Championship 2001 in Ethiopia, CAF
    denied both teams points for the match; however, then the hosts
    had been the intended victims and not the perpetrators.

NB: Togo withdrew after an attack by Cabinda rebel forces on their
    team bus on January 8th killed an assistant trainer and a press
    officer, and seriously injured several players, in spite of the
    bus being accompanied by Angolan armed forces (whose presence
    presumably provoked the attack; these Angolan forces apparently 
    sustained no casualties but miserably failed in protecting their
    guests); the local organisation committee and CAF issued some 
    half-hearted condoleances, at the same time reproaching Togo for
    travelling by bus (they came from a training camp in nearby 
    Congo-Brazzaville) and held a 20-second "minute" of silence before 
    the opening match of the tournament two days after the attack
    (no black armbands or anything).  On January 30th, the day before
    the final, the CAF executive committee saw fit to ban Togo from
    the next two editions of the African championship for 'government
    interference' and fined them USD 50,000.  In contrast, CAF (or
    FIFA) have not been in any hurry to deal with the attack by
    Egyptian fans (unhindered by the local police which accompanied
    the bus from the airport to the hotel) on the Algerian team bus
    (injuring several players) two days prior to their final group
    match in the qualifying tournament on November 14, 2009 in Cairo.
